Coffee, of course

Single origin from Assembly Coffee, roasted down the road in Brixton, is on the bar every day, with a rotating guest on filter.

To take home there are bags from Curve, Hasbean, Dark Arts, Caravan and Round Hill, and the kit to brew them. At the counter: sourdough, fresh pastry, cakes (some of them ours), freshly made lunches to take away, and Hackney Gelato in summer.

Ask about the tables

“If a customer asks ‘Where do you get your beautiful tables from?’ I take the opportunity to tell them they’re made by young people at Goldfinch…”

Steve, assistant manager, talking to Assembly Coffee, 2022

Lumberjack opened in January 2016, set up by London Reclaimed, a South East London employment charity started in 2011 by local youth worker Mike Biddulph.

The idea was simple. Young people who couldn’t get a first job without experience would get paid work making real things for real customers. At Goldfinch that meant joinery. At Lumberjack it meant hospitality: a twelve-month traineeship on the floor and in the kitchen, in customer service and on the coffee machine.

Why “Lumberjack”? “Goldfinch works with a lot of reclaimed wood, hence the name Lumberjack.” Tash, 2022.

  • “It’s a catch-22 where if you haven’t got experience, you can’t get experience. We want to break that cycle…”

    Ann-Marie Yiannis, Lumberjack’s first manager, in the Peckham Peculiar, 2016

  • “This (paid) trainee job has helped me gain confidence and experience in hospitality.”

    Naliah, trainee at Lumberjack, in Southwark News, 2023

  • “People buy furniture because it’s good. They go into Lumberjack because it’s nice.”

    Mike Biddulph, founder of London Reclaimed, in Southwark News, 2023

Since 2016

Drag along the rule

  1. 16 Jan 2016

    Doors open in the old House gallery and café on Church Street. “Our window was smashed the first night after we opened, and then we were broken into the following night.”

  2. 2016

    Time Out drops in: Wood is good. The coffee then came from Old Spike in Peckham.

  3. 2018

    The Camberwell Arts summer exhibition hangs on Lumberjack’s walls.

  4. By 2019

    Assembly Coffee from Brixton is on the bar. The Infatuation: has all the things you want from a great little coffee spot.

  5. 2020

    Lockdown. The café becomes a deli with free delivery within half a mile, and customers buy breakfasts and lunches for NHS staff at King’s.

  6. 2021

    A local illustrator showcase for the Camberwell Arts festival in June. In October, a refit adds more seating downstairs.

  7. 2026

    Thatsup ranks it second of Camberwell’s best cafés: it fuses a coffee shop, deli, and curated retail space into one lively spot.

Upstairs, downstairs, out the back

Light comes in through the big front window, the counter and the deli are upstairs, and most of the seats are below.

  1. Upstairs

    The counter, the A-frame shelving, the deli, and a back room with local art on the walls.

  2. Downstairs

    Down the spiral stair is where most of the seats are. You can pretty much always find a spot downstairs, says The Infatuation, and there are plenty of plugs.

  3. Out the back

    A small back garden, with seats when the weather’s kind.

Dogs welcome. One of the managers, in 2022: “as someone who wants the shop to be full of dogs all day, every day…”
